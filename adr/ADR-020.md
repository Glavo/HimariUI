# ADR-020: Make measure-time structural materialization an explicit contract

- **Status:** Accepted
- **Date:** 2026-08-15
- **Decision milestone:** M1 / RUNTIME-ADR-001
- **Evidence:** The grouped, one-shot, and hybrid viewport fixtures; `evidence/m1-runtime-decision`; ADR-023; and `./gradlew -g .gradle-user-home :spikes:runtime-decision:conformance`.
- **Supersedes:** None
- **Superseded by:** None

## Context

Viewport-driven containers know which children should exist only after constraints and available size are known, while the normal frame flow orders ordinary structure before measure. The M1 comparison therefore tested both a current-viewport structural operation initiated from measure and a previous-viewport scheme with a documented one-frame lag.

## Decision

Use explicitly scoped current-measure structural materialization.

Only a layout container's declared materialization scope may create, remove, or reorder descendants during measure. The scope executes with the current constraints, viewport, stable state epoch, ambient scope, and UI-attempt identity. It may reconcile only its own semantic-keyed descendants and must stage topology, local ownership, dependency edges, and property targets in the attempt's `UiCommitTransaction`. A successful result becomes visible before placement; a failure or cooperative cancellation discards all new descendants and preserves the previously committed viewport.

Reads of constraints and viewport geometry are measure dependencies. Reactive reads retain their normal structural or phase ownership, and the committed materialization records both measure and structure impact. Survivor identity is semantic-keyed; unkeyed reorder is invalid. Effects for new descendants cannot run from inside measure and mount only after commit. Cleanup for removed descendants follows the selected child-before-parent order.

The scope is bounded and non-reentrant: it cannot invoke arbitrary ancestor structure, start an independent commit, perform unmanaged side effects, or recursively materialize itself. Nested lazy containers may enter their own scopes only through normal descendant measurement with independent depth and work budgets. Headless execution uses the same current-viewport result and retry behavior.

The previous-viewport one-frame-lag scheme remains a rejected baseline alternative. It may be reconsidered only as an explicit latency/performance mode under a replacement ADR; it cannot arise silently as overload fallback.

## Consequences

`LazyList` and related containers can compute the visible semantic keys from the current measure constraints and commit those children for placement in the same frame. They must retain anchor-relative or double cumulative scroll coordinates under ADR-009, preserve survivor-local state, apply configured overscan and work budgets, and diagnose duplicate keys or recursive materialization.

The phase scheduler keeps its normal order. This ADR adds one narrow measure-owned structural subphase rather than general subcomposition. A materialization failure leaves the last committed children available for deterministic fallback and requires an explicit retry or later valid invalidation; it cannot expose a half-updated viewport or run effects twice.

## Evidence

The frozen capability-dependent fixture verifies current-viewport and previous-viewport alternatives against the same keyed-survivor state, phase instrumentation, failed-item cleanup, deterministic retry, and realistic application gate. The grouped and hybrid candidates both materialize the current viewport atomically; the selected grouped candidate reconciles visible semantic keys in one private draft, retains a surviving local cell, emits measure and structure attribution, discards a deliberately failed next-item draft, and retries deterministically. The one-shot candidate proves that the rejected lagged alternative is implementable but exposes pending keys until the next frame.

All three candidate suites pass on the JVM and inside the shared Native Image executable. The reviewed decision selects grouped structural recomposition through the frozen score and tie-break sequence, so its scoped-measure capability becomes the production contract. Complete reports and compact decision artifacts are regenerated by `:spikes:runtime-decision:conformance`.

## Replacement

Replace this ADR if production layout evidence shows that bounded current-measure materialization cannot meet accepted frame, recursion, or memory budgets without compromising correctness. A replacement must compare an explicit alternative against current-viewport identity, latency, failure atomicity, phase attribution, Headless determinism, and accessibility behavior; it may not introduce unrestricted or emergent subcomposition.
